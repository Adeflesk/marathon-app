# Keep the lifts, fix Tuesday, load the ankle

**Verdict: you are mostly doing the right thing, and nothing in your routine is harmful.** Two strength sessions a week is the dose the research supports. Your Thursday leg day already uses the right kinds of exercise: a squat-type lift, a hip hinge, single-leg work, straight-knee and bent-knee calf raises, eversion work and single-leg balance. Your taper rules match what coaches advise. Five things should change. **First**, stop rotating the main leg exercise every week; keep it fixed for a block of 3–6 weeks so you can add weight and track progress. **Second**, make Thursday properly heavy. As the app is coded now, Thursday is automatically cut to the light version every week from week 8, because Wednesday's quality run counts as an "adjacent hard session". So the 3 × 6–8 Build prescription never actually happens. **Third**, add a small, staged amount of hopping. Plyometrics are the one proven ingredient you have none of, and they appear to help most at speeds like your race pace. **Fourth**, turn Tuesday from an upper-body day into a lighter full-body and ankle session. No study links upper-body strength to marathon performance. **Fifth**, do much more ankle work. Your balance work is about a minute a week, while the programmes that cut re-sprains used 60–90 minutes a week. The redesigned sessions, the ankle tests and the easy/right/hard progression rules for the app are below. Each claim is labelled **[Sources]** when published evidence or named coaches support it and **[Inferred]** when it is my reasoning from those sources. Most of the app rules are inferred, because no study has tested a three-button rating system.

## What this rests on, and where your routine already matches

**[Sources]** The case for lifting at all is solid. Across 24 studies, strength training added to running improved running economy (the oxygen cost of holding a pace) by **about 2–8%** compared with running alone ([Blagrove et al. 2018](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/journal_contribution/Effects_of_strength_training_on_the_physiological_determinants_of_middle-_and_long-distance_running_performance_a_systematic_review/9619553)). A 2024 meta-analysis found the benefit comes from **heavy loads and plyometrics**, not light "endurance-style" circuits or holds. Plyometrics were significant only at speeds of **12 km/h or below**, which covers your 3:59 pace of about 10.6 km/h. Programmes ran 1–4 sessions a week, and frequency did not change the size of the effect ([Llanos-Lagos et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11052887/)). Across sport in general, strength training cut injuries to **about a third (RR 0.315)**, balance training to about half (RR 0.55), and stretching had no effect ([Lauersen et al. 2014, BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/48/11/871)). Those injury trials were mostly team sports and military groups, not runners.

Measured against that evidence, these parts of your routine are already right and should stay. Two sessions a week matches both the trials and the main published plan, the Runner's World 16-week plan, which uses two total-body sessions a week ([Runner's World SA](https://www.runnersworld.co.za/training/how-to-add-strength-training-to-your-marathon-or-ultra-plan/)). Your Thursday exercise choices cover the families the evidence rewards. Calf and soleus work is the most runner-specific gap in typical gym routines, and you already have it. A 14-week calf and tendon programme in runners cut the energy cost of running by **4%** ([Bohm et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7893283/)). Your ankle exercise *choices* (calf raises, banded eversion and inversion, single-leg balance with ball catches, eyes closed) match the middle phases of a hospital ankle-sprain protocol ([Mass General Brigham](https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/content/dam/unified-xwalk/pdf/patient-education/english/rehabilitation-protocol-for-ankle-sprain.pdf)). Your phase table has the right shape: heavier and lower-rep in Build, a smaller maintenance dose in Peak, and sets cut in cutback weeks. Your taper rules fit coach consensus, which is to end heavy work about 2–3 weeks out and keep only light work after that ([RunnersConnect](https://runnersconnect.net/running-questions/when-should-i-stop-strength-training-at-the-gym-before-the-marathon/)). Lifting on non-running days, with legs on Thursday about 72 hours before the Sunday long run, follows the common rule of no strength within 24 hours of a long run ([Runner's World SA](https://www.runnersworld.co.za/training/how-to-add-strength-training-to-your-marathon-or-ultra-plan/)).

A note on limits. **runnersworld.com (US and UK) and Reddit blocked the research tools**, so every Runner's World plan quoted here comes from the **South African edition**, which republishes US articles. No Reddit, LetsRun or Fetcheveryone threads could be read, so community experience rests on a few forum posts and is thin. Exercise lists from Pfitzinger, Hansons, Fitzgerald's books and Dicharry's *Running Rewired* were not available online, and nothing below is attributed to them. Several primary papers could only be read as abstracts or summaries. Most trials used well-trained runners rather than four-hour marathoners.

## Five gaps between your routine and the evidence

**Weekly rotation undermines progress.** **[Sources]** A review of exercise variation found that planned changes between blocks are fine, but frequent, random variation looked neutral to harmful ([Kassiano et al. 2022 summary](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-7/exercise-variation-for-strength-and-hypertrophy-change-is-not-always-good/)). A running coach lists "constantly changing routines" as one of the four most common strength mistakes runners make, and advises fixed blocks of several weeks with load added each week ([Canadian Running](https://runningmagazine.ca/sections/training/4-things-your-strength-program-is-getting-wrong/)). **[Inferred]** Your knee-dominant slot cycles through four exercises, so each one comes back only every fourth week. You can't add weight week to week. You relearn the balance of the split squat and step-up each time. And every new movement brings fresh soreness two days before Saturday's run. Rotation also makes the app's planned "suggest next time's weight" feature impossible for that slot.

**Thursday is never heavy after week 7.** This comes from reading the app's code (`app/strength.py`), not from a source. Strength A switches to its "reduced" prescription whenever a hard run falls the day before or after. From week 8 onward every Wednesday is a hard session, so every Thursday from week 8 to week 18 gets 2 × 8 at RPE 6 (2 × 6 at RPE 6 in Peak). **[Sources]** That is exactly the light, fatiguing-but-not-heavy zone the evidence rates lowest. Coaches warn against replacing heavy lower-body work with light loads ([FitPro](https://www.fitpro.com/blog/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/11355)), and Blagrove favours low reps with heavy weights ([Pogo Physio podcast page](https://www.pogophysio.com.au/blog/dr-richard-blagrove-rewired/)). The 24-hour concern in the research runs the other way. Hard running was still impaired 24 hours *after* lower-body lifting ([Runner's World AU, Doma](https://runnersworldonline.com.au/study-shows-best-combine-strength-training-running/)), and several coaches put lifting on or just after hard days so that the following days stay easy ([Runner's World SA, Susan Paul](https://www.runnersworld.co.za/training/how-often-should-i-strength-train/)). **[Inferred]** Thursday comes after the quality run and 72 hours before the long run, which makes it the right day to lift heavy. It should only be trimmed when Wednesday actually went badly.

**There is no reactive or hopping work.** **[Sources]** Plyometrics are, alongside heavy lifting, the best-supported way to improve economy. In recreational runners, single-leg and two-leg plyometrics each improved economy by **about 5%** and 3 km time-trial speed by **about 2.5%** over 11 weeks ([Greenwood et al. 2020](https://doaj.org/article/f46c52ef5c4e473ea46b08ebb725be65)). The ankle protocols also make hopping a required stage before full return ([Mass General Brigham](https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/content/dam/unified-xwalk/pdf/patient-education/english/rehabilitation-protocol-for-ankle-sprain.pdf)). Running is, after all, repeated single-leg hopping.

**Tuesday spends its time on the least-supported work.** **[Sources]** The economy and injury benefits in the reviews come almost entirely from lower-body heavy lifting and plyometrics ([Llanos-Lagos 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11052887/)). Arguments for upper-body work (posture, arm swing, breathing) appear only in magazine pieces that cite no trials ([Runner's World AU](https://runnersworldonline.com.au/guide-to-strength-training-upper-body/)). Even the Runner's World plan builds every session as total-body, with a push and a pull as just two of several categories ([Runner's World SA](https://www.runnersworld.co.za/training/how-to-add-strength-training-to-your-marathon-or-ultra-plan/)). Blagrove adds that compound lifts reduce the need for separate core work ([podcast summary](https://de.player.fm/1BaFHwS)). **[Inferred]** Barbell bench, overhead press, kettlebell swings and med-ball slams take up most of Tuesday's 35 minutes and buy little for a marathon. A 16 kg swing is conditioning more than power for most adults, and a 5 kg slam is mostly cardio. That time is better spent on a second, lighter dose of legs, calves and ankle work. One caution is that Tuesday is the day before quality, so it must stay moderate and hop-free.

**The ankle dose is far below anything studied.** **[Sources]** Balance programmes worked best at **3 sessions a week of 20–30 minutes for 4–6 weeks** ([Tang et al. 2024](https://doaj.org/article/e4a9d07e3fab44ea957f72081cd6b5af)). The classic McKeon programme used 12 sessions of 20–25 minutes over 4 weeks ([NCT01790581](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01790581)), and the eccentric eversion trial trained every day for six weeks ([Uludag trial](https://uu245-102.uludag.edu.tr/items/7f4db1ac-6e8e-450f-a1b3-cd4303563feb/full)). Rehab exercise cut the odds of another sprain by **43% (OR 0.57)**, and by **52% in studies with more than 900 minutes** of total exercise ([Bleakley, BMJ](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5112177)). An unsupervised 8-week home balance programme cut re-sprains by about 35% ([Hupperets et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6044595/table/T3)). Your current balance work is 2 × 30 s per leg once a week, roughly 1–2 minutes. Your calf work also stays in the 10–15 rep endurance range, with no test to show when you are ready for more.

## The redesigned week, phase by phase

**[Inferred]** Each session is built from the sources' minimum effective dose: one heavy two-leg lift, one hinge, one single-leg lift, loaded calves, a few hops and a little trunk work. Practitioner sessions are usually 20–30 minutes ([Run Weekly](https://www.runweekly.com/the-minimum-effective-dose-of-strength-training-for-runners/)). Yours are longer only because of the ankle work. In the plan as it stands, Rebuild is weeks 1–3, Build is weeks 4–6, 8–10 and 12–14, Cutback is weeks 7, 11 and 15, Peak is weeks 16–18, Taper is weeks 19 and 20, and race week is week 21. New exercises start in cutback weeks (7 and 11), when loads are lighter. **Nothing new is introduced after week 11**, because trying new exercises in the taper is "the quickest way to get sore" ([Shift to Strength](https://www.shifttostrength.com/post/marathon-taper-guide)).

## The ankle progression and its readiness tests

> **General information, not medical advice.** The grade of your sprain is unknown. If you have ongoing swelling, a feeling of the ankle giving way, pain deep in the joint or behind the outer ankle bone, or progress that stalls, see a physio. They can place you at the right stage and rule out problems that a sprain can hide.

**[Sources]** The 2021 US physiotherapy guideline gives its strongest grade (A) to balance and proprioception exercise after a first sprain and for chronic instability, but it sets no dose ([JOSPT CPG 2021](https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/jospt.2021.0302.pdf)). The same guideline names the tests worth tracking: single-leg balance with eyes closed, reach tests in several directions, ankle bend (dorsiflexion) range, and timed single-leg hopping. The Mass General Brigham protocol orders the work as follows. Band and calf work and single-leg stance come first. Next come multidirectional reaches, ball-toss balance, two-leg hopping and lateral bounding, with running starting in that phase. Single-leg hopping and changes of direction come last ([Mass General Brigham](https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/content/dam/unified-xwalk/pdf/patient-education/english/rehabilitation-protocol-for-ankle-sprain.pdf)). Because you are already running, you are probably in that third phase. Bracing also has strong evidence for preventing a repeat sprain and is worth considering for trail or uneven-ground runs ([Doherty et al., BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/51/2/113)).

**Hop stages.** **[Inferred]** The order of the stages comes from the protocols. The contact counts come from coaching guides, which disagree (beginner ranges run from 30–60 to 80–100 per session) ([Find Your Edge](https://www.findyouredge.app/news/plyometric-training-for-runners); [RunBikeCalc](https://runbikecalc.com/blog/plyometrics-runners-complete-guide-2026)). Stay at each stage for at least two weeks.

**[Sources]** Start with one hop session a week for 2–3 weeks before adding a second ([Outside](https://www.outsideonline.com/running/training/workouts/ready-for-power-building-plyometrics-take-this-test-and-start-with-these-exercises/?scope=anon)). Plyometrics can go at the start of a strength session or before an easy run or speed session ([Runner's World SA](https://www.runnersworld.co.za/training/how-to-add-strength-training-to-your-marathon-or-ultra-plan/)). **[Inferred]** Thursday is the main hop day. From Stage 1, a second, smaller dose of 2 × 15 pogos can go into the warm-up for Wednesday's quality run.

**Ankle snacks.** **[Inferred]** On three to five other days, do 8–10 minutes of balance work after an easy run or at home: eyes-closed stance, Y-balance reaches, band perturbations (a band round the waist pulling you sideways) and eccentric eversion. Counting Tuesday and Thursday, that brings weekly ankle time to about 40–60 minutes, close to the studied doses. Over a 21-week build it adds up to roughly the 900 cumulative minutes linked with the largest drop in re-sprains ([Bleakley, BMJ](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5112177)). Balance progressions run from eyes open, to eyes closed, to ball catches, to band perturbations, to Y-balance reaches, to a single-leg RDL with a reach, to McKeon-style hop-and-stick in each direction ([McKeon protocol](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01790581)).

**Stop-or-step-back signals.** **[Sources]** A widely used pain-monitoring model treats 0–2/10 as safe and up to 5/10 as acceptable during exercise, provided pain is back to baseline by the next morning ([University of Antwerp](https://www.uantwerpen.be/en/research-groups/movant/blog/provokepain/)). The protocol requires "no increase in pain or swelling" with hopping ([MGB](https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/content/dam/unified-xwalk/pdf/patient-education/english/rehabilitation-protocol-for-ankle-sprain.pdf)). This model was developed mainly for tendon problems, not sprains. **[Inferred]** Drop back one stage if any of these appear: next-morning swelling or pain above baseline, an episode of giving way, a calf-raise count that falls between tests, or wobbly or noisy landings. Get the ankle assessed if pain sits deep in the joint, at the front or inner side, or behind the outer ankle bone, or if any signal lasts more than 1–2 weeks.

## Progression rules for the app's logging card

**[Inferred]** No source has tested a three-level easy/right/hard rating. The rules below put together three tested ideas. First, the reps-in-reserve scale: people usually judge it to within about one rep, most accurately in sets of 12 or fewer ([Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/reps-in-reserve)). Second, double progression: hold a weight until every set reaches the top of the rep range, then add the smallest increment ([Setgraph](https://setgraph.app/articles/double-progression-the-smarter-way-to-add-reps-and-weight)). Third, a published fixed-kilogram rule: +2.5 to +5 kg after an easy set, no change on target, and −2.5 to −5 kg when too hard ([JOHK](https://johk.pl/?p=5643)). The textbook "2-for-2" rule (go up after beating the target by 2 reps in two sessions) and its suggested 5–10% jumps for lower-body lifts point the same way ([Achievable CSCS](https://app.achievable.me/study/cscs/learn/program-design-for-resistance-training-training-frequency-exercise-order-and-training-load-and-repetitions)). The numbers will need tuning against your real logs.

**What the card records.** For each exercise: the weight, pre-filled with the suggestion and editable; the reps done in each set, pre-filled with the target; one rating after the last working set; and, on calf, hop, balance and single-leg exercises, an **ankle pain** flag (0–10), kept separate from effort. Bodyweight, band and ring exercises record a level instead of a weight (band colour, foot position, stage). The card should show the "next time" suggestion as soon as you rate, so you can see why it changed.

**Rating meanings**

| Button | Meaning | Reps left in the tank |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | Could have done 4 or more extra reps with good form | ≥4 (RPE ≤6) |
| Right | 2–3 reps left; the last reps slowed but form held | 2–3 (RPE 7–8) |
| Hard | 0–1 left, form broke down, or target reps were missed | ≤1 (RPE ≥9) |

**Rating to next session**

| Rating | All sets reached the top of the range? | Next time |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | Yes | +1 load step and back to the bottom of the range. If last session was also easy, +2 steps. |
| Easy | No | Same load, +2 reps per set (capped at the top). If within 1 rep of the top, +1 step and back to the bottom. |
| Right | Yes | +1 load step, back to the bottom of the range |
| Right | No | Same load, aim for +1 rep per set |
| Hard | Target reps completed | Repeat the same load and reps |
| Hard | Reps missed, or a second hard in a row | −1 load step (about 5–10%), reps at the bottom of the range |
| Any, with ankle pain ≥3 (or higher than last time) | — | No increase on any ankle, calf or hop item; repeat or step back one stage. A next-morning reaction overrides the rating and drops one stage. |

**Load steps for your equipment**

| Equipment | Step | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Barbell, lower body (box squat, RDL, hip thrust, seated soleus) | +5 kg below about 60 kg; +2.5 kg above 60 kg and in Peak | Your plates (one pair each of 1.25–20 kg) make every total from 20 to 127.5 kg in 2.5 kg steps. The app's `barbell_loads()` already calculates this. |
| Barbell, upper body (if bench is kept) | +2.5 kg | Under 50 kg that is a 5%+ jump, so use a wider rep range (6–10) to absorb it |
| Dumbbells | +1.25 kg per hand on upper body and calves; +1.25–2.5 kg per hand on split squats, step-ups, lunges and single-leg RDL | 2.5–35 kg in 1.25 kg steps |
| Kettlebells (12 → 16 kg) | Only one jump, and it is 33% | Move up only after "easy" at the top of an extended range (e.g. swings 3 × 15–20), then restart at 3 × 8–10. Otherwise use a dumbbell version with finer steps. |
| Bodyweight, rings, bands, box | Reps → tempo → leverage or level | Ring row: feet further forward. Push-up → ring push-up. Thicker band. Higher step. |

**At the top weight** (16 kg kettlebell, 35 kg dumbbells, or a barbell jump that's too big). Each time you rate "right" at the top of the range, move one rung down this ladder. **[Sources]** The levers come from home-gym coaching guides, which also warn that real strength gains eventually need more load ([kettlebellsworkouts.com](https://kettlebellsworkouts.com/ways-progress-your-kettlebell-workouts/); [BarBend](https://barbend.com/tempo-train-kettlebells/)).

| Rung | Lever | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Extend the rep range by up to 4 (main lifts no higher than 10–12; accessories up to 15–20) | Bulgarian split squat with 2 × 35 kg: 3 × 8 → 3 × 12 |
| 2 | 3–4 s lowering, reps back to the bottom | Split squat 3 × 6 with a 4 s descent |
| 3 | 2–3 s pause at the hardest point | Pause at the bottom of the split squat or mid-shin on the RDL |
| 4 | Harder variation | Step-up → higher box; two-arm swing → one-arm swing; calf raise → off a plate (deficit) |
| 5 | Move the lift to the barbell | Seated soleus or calf raise loaded with the bar instead of dumbbells |
| Last resort | Shorter rest | Avoid: it adds fatigue rather than strength |

Two examples for your kit. Farmer's carry at 2 × 35 kg goes from 40 m to 60 m, then to a one-hand suitcase carry with 35 kg. Kettlebell swings at 16 kg go to 3 × 20, then to one-arm swings.

**Phase and check-in overrides** (checked before the rating rules)

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Cutback week | No load increases. Drop sets by a third to a half, keep the last "right" load. Record ratings, but act only on "hard". |
| Peak | Main leg lifts hold their load and cap at RPE 7. Accessories and upper body may still progress. |
| Taper | No increases. Taper-2 primer at 70–80% of the last working load. |
| Race week | No strength card, only the balance snack. |
| Check-in factor 0.9 | −1 set on main lifts, same load |
| Check-in factor 0.8 | −1 set and −1 load step, cap RPE 6, no increases that week |
| Wednesday rated hard | Thursday: −1 set on main lifts, cap RPE 7 |
| Ankle flag | No hop or calf progression; hops drop one stage if there is a next-morning reaction |

These follow the plan's "only adjusts down" rule. In reduced sessions an "easy" rating never raises the load.

**Return after time off.** **[Sources]** Two to three weeks off costs little strength (about 1% on average in one summary), but technique fades first, so restart lighter and avoid big soreness ([Jeff Nippard](https://jeffnippard.com/blogs/news/how-to-get-your-gains-back-after-a-training-break)). No source gives percentages, so the numbers here are **[Inferred]**. After a gap of up to 10 days, resume at the last load. After 2–3 weeks, do one session at about 90% (1–2 steps down), then go back to the rating rules. After more than 4 weeks, or after an ankle flare, restart the Rebuild prescription at about 80% of the last load, and redo the ankle tests before restarting hops.

**Starting loads.** **[Inferred]** You don't need a max test. In the first session of each new exercise, the app shows 3–4 warm-up sets climbing from an easy weight: the empty 20 kg bar for barbell lifts, a light pair of dumbbells for dumbbell work, bodyweight for calves. Stop at the weight where the bottom of the rep range feels like about 3–4 reps left (RPE 6–7), and log that as the working weight. Rebuild's RPE-6 target and the "easy means +1 or +2 steps" rule then correct any early under-guess within 2–4 sessions. New block exercises in weeks 7 and 11 start the same way. A load-and-reps estimate of your one-rep max (Epley formula) can sanity-check the numbers, but shouldn't drive the prescription.

## Conclusion

The fixes are mostly about where things go, not new equipment or more time. Moving about 15 minutes of Tuesday's pressing, swinging and slamming into single-leg, calf and ankle work, and letting Thursday be truly heavy, turns a routine that only resembles the evidence into one that delivers it. The biggest single gain is the one you can't see in the exercise list. Right now the leg day the evidence rewards most is cut to its light version every week from week 8, so fixing the "adjacent hard run" rule matters more than any change of exercise.

The logging card has a second job. The research gives clear direction but few validated numbers for a four-hour marathoner coming back from a sprain. The 25-rep calf gate, the contact counts, the easy/right/hard thresholds and the time-off percentages are all reasonable starting points. Your own logs over the next 21 weeks will show whether they suit you. Treat the first block as calibration. Calf-raise counts and Y-balance symmetry that rise between tests are the best evidence available that the ankle work is doing its job.
